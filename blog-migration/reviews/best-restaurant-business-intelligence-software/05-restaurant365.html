5. Restaurant365

Category: Food and labor cost intelligence

Restaurant365 puts the general ledger at the center of the restaurant back office, with stock, recipe costs, schedules, payroll and reports wrapped around it in the cloud. Its AI engine, R365 AI, reads the complete P&L, which makes it a natural pick for finance-led groups that want their BI to begin with the books.

Notable customers: Villa Restaurant Group, Wholly Stromboli and Johnson's Landing.

What's new: R365 AI debuted at the 2026 National Restaurant Association Show in May. The launch covered dashboards and an advisor powered by AI, plus a new labor suite, while AI-built schedules are still limited to early-access customers. Its customer success push continued in June with the hire of Kate Hastings into the CCO role.

Key features:

  • Restaurant-grade accounting. Supplier invoices get processed, bank accounts reconciled and P&Ls produced for each entity automatically, so the finance numbers behind your dashboards stay current.
  • Food cost variance. Counts and recipe costs reveal the gap between the theoretical food cost and what the kitchen actually used.
  • Automatic sales journals. Connected POS systems post daily sales to the ledger, so nobody rekeys totals from Z reports.
  • Labor tied to forecasts. Scheduling and labor tools work from sales forecasts, and payroll handles hourly teams and tips.
  • Group-level budgets and reporting. Multi-location reports and budgets cover the whole group, including royalty tracking for franchise operators.
  • An AI advisor on the P&L. The R365 AI advisor and its dashboards flag cost, labor and margin issues that deserve a manager's attention.

Pricing: Bundles are priced by location and invoiced every quarter or every year; bigger groups receive a custom quote. Because the vendor's pages quote conflicting figures, get the current number from the pricing page itself.
