5. Restaurant365

Category: Food and labor cost analytics

Restaurant365 puts the accounting, inventory, recipe costing, scheduling, payroll and reporting of a restaurant group into one cloud back office. Because operations data posts straight to the P&L, your cost analytics and your books draw on the same numbers. Its newer AI layer, R365 AI, is built on that P&L data.

Notable customers: Villa Restaurant Group, Wholly Stromboli and Johnson's Landing.

What's new: Restaurant365 unveiled R365 AI in May 2026 at the National Restaurant Association's annual show. It reads the whole P&L and adds dashboards and an advisor driven by AI, a suite for managing labor, and AI-built schedules (that last piece is still in early access). In June 2026, the company gave the Chief Customer Officer role to Kate Hastings.

Key features:

  • Food cost gaps. With counts and recipe costs in the system, you can compare what dishes should have used with what was actually consumed, which points you to waste and over-portioning.
  • Accounting built for restaurants. Invoice processing, bank reconciliation and P&Ls for multiple entities run automatically, so cost reports reflect real spend.
  • Daily sales in the books. Sales flow in from the POS overnight, which keeps margin reports up to date with nobody keying in totals.
  • Labor planned against forecasts. Scheduling ties shifts to sales forecasts, and payroll covers hourly teams and tips, so labor cost analysis rests on the actual pay data.
  • Group-level reporting and AI insights. Multi-location budgets, franchise royalty tracking and R365 AI dashboards highlight P&L, labor and cost trends across stores.

Pricing: Bundles are priced by location and invoiced each quarter or year; bigger groups get a tailored quote. Dollar amounts differ between Restaurant365's own pages, so check its pricing page for today's rate.
