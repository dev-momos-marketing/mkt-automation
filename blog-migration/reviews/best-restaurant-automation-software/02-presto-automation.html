2. Presto Automation

Website: Presto Automation

Category: Automated drive-thru order-taking with voice AI

With Presto Voice, a quick-service chain hands the drive-thru speaker to software that talks to each guest and keys the order into the register, using the crew headsets already installed. The company behind it has changed hands: after Nasdaq delisted Presto Automation, a Remus Capital-led buyer group took over its assets at the end of 2024 and operates it privately under the name Presto Phoenix. Restaurants already using the product stayed on through the sale.

Notable customers: Carl's Jr. / Hardee's and Taco John's.

What's new: Presto closed a $10 million round in January 2026, with Metropolitan Partners Group leading, and said the money would expand large-chain deployments now that the product is live in hundreds of lanes. During 2026 Presto became a Toast partner too, which means a Toast store can send orders taken by the voice assistant on to its kitchen screens.

Key features:

  • Speaker-post ordering. An AI voice greets each car, takes the full order and passes it on, which frees a crew member from the headset during the rush.
  • Works with current hardware. Orders flow through your existing headsets and POS, and Toast users get a direct integration.
  • Upsell on every car. The assistant suggests add-ons during the conversation, so the prompt never depends on how busy the window is.
  • Multilingual lanes. A guest who prefers another language can switch, and the assistant keeps taking the order at that speaker.
  • One menu for every store. Menu changes are managed centrally and pushed out across all your locations.

What makes them stand out: Presto is built only for the drive-thru lane, and its September 2025 partnership with ElevenLabs brought more natural-sounding synthetic voices, including custom voices that match a brand.

Pricing: No list price is posted; Presto prices each chain after a sales conversation.

It's a great fit for: Multi-unit QSR chains and franchise groups whose drive-thru lanes are busy enough that automating order-taking pays back in labor.
