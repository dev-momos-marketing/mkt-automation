6. Crunchtime

Crunchtime is an enterprise restaurant operations suite that large franchise systems use to run food cost, labor and store execution from the center. It folded in Zenput, now named Crunchtime Ops Execution, and its 2025 merger with QSR Automations brought kitchen display and host products into the same family.

Notable customers: P.F. Chang's, Gong cha, Panini Kabob Grill and Smoothie King.

What's new: In April 2026, Crunchtime added four AI capabilities, among them stock counts by voice using speech-to-text. In September 2026 it launched a connected kitchen and operations suite that links its back office with Crunchtime Kitchen and Crunchtime Host, the renamed QSR Automations products.

Key features:

  • Recipe-level food cost. Counts, ordering and recipe costing raise variance alerts, so you can spot waste at a particular store.
  • Counting by voice. Managers speak counts into the app, which turns them into text and shortens stock takes.
  • Forecasts that drive labor. Sales forecasts feed both crew schedules and replenishment orders.
  • Compliant scheduling. Schedules account for labor law rules across your locations.
  • Store execution. Checklists, audits, tasks and food safety come from the former Zenput product.
  • Kitchen and host tools. Crunchtime Kitchen runs kitchen displays and Crunchtime Host manages waitlists and tables.

What makes them stand out: The inventory engine follows usage down to each recipe, flags variances as they appear and uses sales forecasts to automate replenishment.

Pricing: Custom enterprise pricing. Crunchtime doesn't publish prices.

It's a great fit for: Large franchise groups where food cost and labor are the main levers, and where above-store leaders need current numbers for dozens or hundreds of units.
