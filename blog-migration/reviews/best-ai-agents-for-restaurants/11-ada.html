11. Ada

Ada sells AI agents that settle customer service conversations on their own, whether a guest writes in by chat, messaging app or email or calls in. Nothing about it is tailored to restaurants, yet a chain buried in questions about refunds, gift cards, app logins and order problems can put it in front of every channel and let staff take only what it can't finish.

Notable customers: Betsson, Cebu Pacific, monday.com and Malaysia Airlines.

What's new: February 2026 brought the Unified Reasoning Engine, so Ada's voice and text agents now think with one shared brain, and Playbooks started working on phone calls too. Five months later, a July 2026 update to the builder added 60-language AI generation, pictures inside automated replies, audit logs for enterprises and extra APIs.

What you can do with Ada:

  • Cover calls and messages alike. Guests reach the identical AI agent whether they phone, email, use web chat or send a message.
  • Walk through multi-step tasks. A Playbook lays out each step for something like refunding a missing side dish, and the agent follows it.
  • Reply in the guest's own language. AI generation is native across 60 languages, useful for brands in several countries.
  • Use pictures in answers. An automated reply might show a screenshot pointing to the loyalty number inside your app.
  • Plug into your stack. Developers can use Ada's APIs to tie the agent to ordering, loyalty or account tools and build on it.
  • Pass tough cases to people. Through helpdesk connections like Dixa, a stuck conversation moves to a human with its history intact.

Why Ada stands out: A single reasoning engine sits behind voice and text, so a guest gets consistent answers whether they call or message. Audit logging gives larger brands a record of what the AI said.

It's best for: Restaurant brands and chains with high support volume that want an AI agent to settle routine guest questions on every channel before staff get involved.

Pricing: No price list is public. Expect a custom quote from Ada's sales team, which mostly prices by how many conversations you run.
