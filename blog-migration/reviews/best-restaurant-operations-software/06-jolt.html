6. Jolt

Jolt moves the paper side of a shift onto a tablet: opening and closing checklists, temperature logs, prep labels and staff schedules. Since August 2025 Jolt has belonged to Digi International, whose SmartSense unit makes temperature sensors, and Jolt still sells under its own name and site.

For a district manager, the main benefit is proof. Each task is logged with a time, a name and often a photo, so you can see which stores completed their food safety checks without visiting them.

Notable customers: A&W Restaurants and White Spot Hospitality.

What's new: At NRF 2026 in January, the SmartSense by Digi team showed SmartSense ONE, which packages Jolt's task software and SmartSense monitoring as seven core modules you can mix. The acquisition by Digi, for about $145.5 million, closed in August 2025.

Key features:

  • Checklists with proof. Staff tick off tasks with photos, and managers sign off, so missed steps show up the same day.
  • Food safety records. Temperature logs are kept digitally, and SmartSense sensors can monitor coolers between manual checks.
  • Printed prep labels. Use-by dates come out of a connected printer, so nobody squints at a smudged marker on a deli container.
  • Rota and punches. Managers drag shifts into place and staff clock in on the same tablet that holds their task lists.
  • Training and reference. A training library and information hub give new hires recipes and procedures on the line.
  • Completion reports. Multi-location reports compare task completion by store and POS links include PAR Brink.

What makes them stand out: Jolt is built around the individual shift, which makes it practical for quick-service teams with high turnover who need clear, short tasks and a record that they were done.

Pricing: Not listed. Jolt's pricing page asks you to talk to sales.

It's a great fit for: QSR brands and franchisees who need to know each shift did its checks, want clean food safety records , and may add SmartSense sensors later.
