16. Legion

Website: Legion

Category: Hourly workforce planning with AI (Labor & Scheduling)

Built for employers with thousands of hourly workers, Legion uses AI throughout its workforce suite: it forecasts demand, builds schedules that respect budgets and labor laws, and handles time, attendance and staff messaging. Retail gets most of the attention on its site these days, though a restaurant edition is still on offer, and the only restaurant case study features a fast casual chain running 60+ units. For big fast food operators in cities with predictive-scheduling rules, the compliance tracking is the main draw.

Notable customers: Wilson James and an unnamed fast-casual chain (60+ restaurants, 6,000+ employees).

What's new: A January 2026 update shipped over 90 additions, headlined by a new wave of AI helpers that take scheduling choices off managers' plates. July 2026 saw the Spring 2026 release reach general availability, adding a schedule assistant aimed at multi-store supervisors (Legion calls it the Upper Field Schedule Assistant). Then in August 2026 came a Legion-commissioned Forrester Total Economic Impact report drawing on four customers in retail and hospitality.

Key features:

  • Demand forecasts. Sales and traffic data drive predictions of how busy each store will be.
  • Auto-built schedules. Shifts are generated to fit the forecast while staying inside the labor budget.
  • Fair Workweek tracking. The system monitors predictive-scheduling rules and calculates premium pay owed for late changes.
  • Clock-ins and a crew app. Time and attendance pairs with a mobile app for open shifts, swaps and availability.
  • Messaging and AI help. Team announcements sit alongside AI assistants that build, explain and translate schedules.
  • Views for area managers. District and area leaders can see schedules across many stores at once.

What makes them stand out: Its compliance tools track Fair Workweek obligations automatically, which matters to fast food brands operating in cities that penalize last-minute schedule changes.

Pricing: Legion doesn't publish pricing; enterprise contracts are quoted individually.

It's a great fit for: Fast food, fast casual and convenience chains employing large crews across many sites, especially where local scheduling laws apply.
