3. Presto Automation

Website: Presto

Category: Voice ordering for the drive-thru lane

Presto Voice listens at the menu board and keys the order for you, using the headset system and register your store already runs. After Presto Automation defaulted on a loan and was delisted, a Remus Capital-led group bought its assets in December 2024; the company carries on privately as Presto Phoenix and kept its existing accounts. Its whole focus is shaving seconds off each car at the speaker.

Notable customers: Carl's Jr. / Hardee's and Taco John's.

What's new: A September 2025 tie-up with ElevenLabs brought warmer synthetic voices to the lane. January 2026 brought a $10 million raise aimed at large QSR deployments, with Presto describing a footprint of hundreds of drive-thru lanes. Later in 2026 a Toast integration followed via the Toast Partner Ecosystem.

Key features:

  • Takes over the headset. The AI greets cars and captures orders, so the person who used to do that can bag food or run the window.
  • Few hardware changes. It works through the headsets and POS you have, and Toast stores get a direct kitchen connection.
  • Suggestive selling every time. Fries, drinks and upgrades are offered on each car, including the ones that arrive at 1 a.m.
  • Multilingual lanes. The assistant takes orders in a number of languages, which helps in neighborhoods with mixed customer bases.
  • Lifelike or custom voices. ElevenLabs speech makes the assistant sound natural, and brands can commission their own voice.
  • Central menu control. Items, prices and availability can be managed for all locations from one place.

What makes them stand out: A drive-thru-only product that now plugs into Toast, giving smaller franchise groups a path in alongside big enterprise chains.

Pricing: Presto prices each contract individually; contact the company for numbers.

It's a great fit for: QSR franchisees and chains whose drive-thru carries most of the volume and whose crews are short on hands at peak.
