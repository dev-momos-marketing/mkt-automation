13. Ada

Ada is an AI customer service platform whose agent answers customers across chat, messaging, email and voice. Brands use it to resolve routine questions automatically and hand the rest to people through the helpdesk they already run.

Ada's 2026 releases center on one agent for every channel: the Unified Reasoning Engine arrived in February, and a July update added native AI generation in 60 languages and images in automated conversations. Customers named on its site include Cebu Pacific, monday.com and Barnes & Noble.

Key features

  • Unified Reasoning Engine: One intelligence layer runs the agent on voice, email, chat, WhatsApp, SMS, Instagram and in-app messages, so answers stay consistent.
  • Playbooks: Structured workflows guide the agent through multi-step requests on voice as well as chat and messaging.
  • Multilingual answers: Native generation in 60 languages lets one agent serve customers in different regions without separate scripts.
  • APIs and MCP: Open APIs, SDKs and MCP support connect the agent to your business systems, so it can look things up and complete tasks.
  • Human handoff: Integrations with Zendesk, Salesforce, Dixa and other helpdesks pass a conversation to your agents when someone needs a person.
  • Audit logging: Enterprise audit logs support security and compliance reviews.

Pros

  • Focused on the AI agent: Ada concentrates on automated service, and the same agent works across text and voice.
  • Fits your stack: It connects to Zendesk, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Genesys and other contact-center tools.
  • Compliance coverage: Ada lists SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR and PCI DSS among its certifications.
  • Rated well: Ada scores 4.6 out of 5 on G2.

Cons

  • No public prices: Pricing comes after a 30-minute call with Ada's team.
  • Volume-based cost: The default model charges for every conversation, so the bill grows with traffic.
  • Handoff through your helpdesk: When a guest needs a person, Ada passes the chat to a helpdesk you connect, such as Zendesk or Salesforce.
  • Enterprise scale: Ada is built for brands with high support volume, so a smaller restaurant group may not have enough conversations to justify it.

Price and plans

Ada doesn't publish prices. Most customers pay per conversation, and a resolution-based model is available for some enterprises; you get a quote after a demo.
