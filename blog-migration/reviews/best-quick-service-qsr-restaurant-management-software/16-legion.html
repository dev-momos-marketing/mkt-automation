16. Legion

Category: AI workforce management (Labor & Scheduling)

Legion builds AI-native workforce management for large hourly teams: it forecasts demand, generates schedules that respect labor rules, and covers time, attendance and team messaging. Its 2026 marketing leans toward retail, though it still sells a restaurant product. For QSR operators, it's an enterprise option for big crews in cities with predictive scheduling laws.

Notable customers: Wilson James and an unnamed fast-casual chain (60+ restaurants, 6,000+ employees).

What's new: In January 2026, Legion shipped more than 90 features, including new AI assistants for scheduling decisions. July 2026 saw the Spring release go live for all customers, including an Upper Field Schedule Assistant that helps multi-store supervisors. In August 2026, it published a Forrester Total Economic Impact study that Legion commissioned.

Key features:

  • Demand forecasting. Traffic counts and sales history feed an AI model of how many people each hour needs.
  • Budget-aware schedules. Draft schedules come out already fitted to your labor dollars.
  • Fair Workweek tracking. Compliance with predictive scheduling laws is monitored, including premium pay owed for late changes.
  • Employee app. Hourly staff claim open shifts, trade with coworkers and edit availability from a phone, which also carries team chat.
  • AI schedule assistants. Assistants build, explain and translate schedules for managers and staff.
  • Multi-store views. Supervisors overseeing several units get one screen for coverage and hours across them.

What makes them stand out: Automation runs deep, from forecast to compliant schedule, which suits operators managing thousands of hourly workers.

Pricing: Custom enterprise pricing; Legion doesn't publish rates.

It's a great fit for: Large multi-unit quick-service, fast-casual, convenience and retail businesses employing thousands of hourly workers under fair workweek laws.
