3. Presto Automation

Category: Drive-thru voice AI

If your management priority is labor at the speaker box, Presto's product, Presto Voice, takes over order capture in the lane while plugging into existing headsets and registers. Ownership changed recently: Presto Automation left Nasdaq, and a Remus Capital-led buyer group acquired its assets at the end of 2024. Operating today as privately held Presto Phoenix with $18M in fresh funding, it kept its installed customer base.

Notable customers: Carl's Jr. / Hardee's and Taco John's.

What's new: September 2025 brought ElevenLabs speech, giving the lane voice a more human sound. A January 2026 round of $10 million, with Metropolitan Partners Group leading, came as Presto reported hundreds of live lanes beyond the pilot stage. It also became a Toast partner during 2026.

Key features:

  • Automated order capture. Guests talk to the AI at the board, freeing your headset crew for bagging, payment and the window.
  • Uses the gear you own. Existing headset systems and POS stay in place, and Toast stores get voice tickets routed to the kitchen.
  • Add-ons offered consistently. The system offers add-ons in each conversation, so suggestive selling holds up during peak hours.
  • Multilingual lanes. One speaker post can serve guests ordering in different languages.
  • Branded voice options. Brands may pick realistic ElevenLabs voices or have a custom one built.
  • Menus managed centrally. Price and item updates are set in one place and reach all enrolled stores.

What makes them stand out: A narrow focus on the drive-thru plus reuse of installed hardware means fewer moving parts during a chainwide rollout.

Pricing: Available by quote from Presto; prices aren't listed publicly.

It's a great fit for: Franchise operators running several high-traffic lanes who want speaker-box ordering handled by AI.
