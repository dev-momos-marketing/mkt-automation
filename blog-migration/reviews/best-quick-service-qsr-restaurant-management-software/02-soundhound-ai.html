2. Soundhound AI

Category: Voice AI for ordering & drive-thru

Within a QSR management stack, SoundHound AI owns the ordering channels where a guest talks instead of taps: incoming calls, the menu board speaker and voice kiosks. The company trades on Nasdaq under SOUN and has bulked up through acquisitions, adding Amelia and SYNQ3 Restaurant Solutions during 2024, then Interactions a year later.

Notable customers: Five Guys, Chipotle, Red Lobster and Applebee's / IHOP, and Church's Texas Chicken.

What's new: In January 2026, after its AI phone line passed one million interactions, Five Guys renewed, and franchisees may now switch the agent on themselves. Four months later, SoundHound introduced OASYS, a self-learning system for designing an agent once and deploying it to calls, lanes, kiosks, chat and vehicles.

Key features:

  • Calls that never ring out. An AI agent picks up each call, writes the order and fields guest questions on allergens, store hours or menu items.
  • Lane ordering. Its drive-thru assistant greets cars at the board, including on Acrelec lane hardware.
  • Tickets fired automatically. Through the POS connection, voice orders reach the kitchen without a cashier retyping anything.
  • Scripted promotions. The agent mentions add-ons and current deals mid-order, keeping your offer cadence identical on every shift.
  • Voice Insights. Lane conversations are turned into operating data that district leaders can compare store by store.

What makes them stand out: Breadth: a single supplier handles calls, lanes, kiosks and in-car ordering, which simplifies vendor management for a large franchise system.

Pricing: Quoted individually; SoundHound keeps its rates private.

It's a great fit for: Big quick-service and casual-dining systems looking to move phone and lane order taking away from crew members at scale.
