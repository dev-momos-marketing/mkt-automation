5. OpenTable

Category: Restaurant reservations & table management software

OpenTable gives a restaurant two things at once: a host-stand system for tables and guests, and a place on a large diner network where people search for somewhere to book. Owned by Booking Holdings, it has spent 2025 and 2026 adding AI features, both for diners asking questions before they reserve and for restaurants trying to show up in AI assistants and search.

The network side matters for management because bookings arrive from outside your own marketing, while confirmations and reminders help keep the book accurate.

Notable customers: Piano Piano (Toronto) and HMSHost (U.S. airport restaurants).

What's new: In August 2026, OpenTable released more than 20 new or updated features, the biggest batch in its history. Highlights for operators include profiles that AI drafts from your own website, Guest AI suggestions, hookups with voice AI vendors, and bookable listings that surface when diners ask Google, ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity or Alexa+. American and Canadian restaurants got them first. Its Concierge assistant, launched in July 2025, was widened a year later, in July 2026, so diners can now describe the meal they want and get matching restaurants.

Key features:

  • Bookings from the network and your site. Diners reserve through OpenTable's apps or through a widget you place on your own website.
  • Floor plans and table management. Hosts seat walk-ins and reservations on a live map of the room.
  • Guest profiles. Visit history and preferences are saved, so your team can recognize regulars and note special requests.
  • Reminders and email marketing. Automatic confirmations and reminders help keep the book accurate, and higher plans add email campaigns.
  • AI answers for diners. Concierge responds to diner questions on your profile, which can save your team some phone calls.
  • Wider distribution and integrations. Bookings can come through AI assistants and search, and OpenTable links up with AI answering services for the phone and with point-of-sale systems including Square.

What makes them stand out: Few reservation tools pair the back-of-house booking system with a consumer network this large, so the same software that runs your floor also brings in new diners.

Pricing: You choose among Basic, Core or Pro. Each carries a monthly fee, and every diner seated from an OpenTable-network booking adds a cover charge on top. It doesn't list current amounts on its site; third-party 2026 summaries quote $149, $299 and $499 per month, so confirm with OpenTable.

Things to consider: Per-cover fees on network bookings grow with volume, so busy venues should model the total monthly cost and compare it with how many diners the network actually sends.

It's a great fit for: Full-service and fine-dining restaurants and groups that depend on reservations and want table, guest and marketing tools together with access to OpenTable's diners.
