2. Toast

Category: Restaurant point of sale (POS) & operations software

Plenty of operators already ring up checks on Toast, a publicly traded company whose POS and management software is made only for restaurants. For an operations lead, its appeal is that the order screen, kitchen tickets, payroll and guest marketing come from a single vendor, so a menu edit or a labor report never has to cross between systems.

By June 30, 2026, Toast counted about 180,000 locations live on its platform, and its AI work now carries the Toast IQ label.

Notable customers: Captain Parker's Pub, Kaldi's Coffee and Craft Lounge.

What's new: Toast's May 2026 spring launch added Toast IQ Grow, a premium tier in which an AI agent runs marketing work with backup from a human success manager, and the Toast Local diner app returned with loyalty baked in. Second-quarter results in August 2026 showed roughly 9,500 locations added net.

Key features:

  • Hardware for each station. Your team can punch in orders at the counter, on a self-order kiosk or on a Toast Go handheld walked out to guests.
  • Screens on the line. A kitchen display system replaces paper chits, so cooks see each ticket and you can watch how long they take.
  • Central menu control. Items and prices get edited once at head office and then go out to every store.
  • People and pay. Payroll, team tools and Sling by Toast scheduling place labor hours right beside sales figures.
  • Repeat-visit marketing. Gift cards, loyalty and email or text campaigns work together with the AI agent included in Toast IQ Grow.
  • Reports across stores. Area managers can line up sites against each other inside Toast instead of merging exports.

What makes them stand out: One system runs the dining room, the kitchen and much of the back office, which means fewer integrations for you to babysit and one support number when something breaks.

Pricing: Toast quotes plans through its sales team. A free Starter Kit and paid plans have appeared on its website, so look at the live pricing page before you budget.

It's a great fit for: Everything from a lone neighborhood spot to a franchise system, as long as you'd like POS, ordering, payroll and guest marketing under one contract.
