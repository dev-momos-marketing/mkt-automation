14. TouchBistro

Category: POS-Based Sales & Performance Reporting

TouchBistro runs on iPads and builds sales reporting right into the POS, with optional modules for payments, online orders, reservations, stock and staff. Harris, an operating group within Constellation Software, bought it in July 2026 and still sells it as TouchBistro. Its reports are useful for sales, menu and staff performance, though the company hasn't announced AI-driven reporting.

Notable customers: Café Crêpe (multi-location group with central commissary), Uncle Tetsu (multi-unit QSR, North America), Koyo (sushi, Queens NY) and Masseria.

What's new: July 2026 saw TouchBistro change hands, with Harris promising to keep developing the POS. The deal terms stayed private, although a Globe and Mail report estimated roughly $100 million.

Key features:

  • Sales sliced three ways. Live reports split revenue by time period, menu item and server, and you can open them from anywhere through the cloud.
  • Menu mix. Item analysis points out which dishes earn their spot and which ones barely move.
  • Shift and server results. Performance reports let you compare one team or server with another.
  • Exceptions tracking. Every discount, void and refund is recorded, which makes suspicious habits easier for a manager to catch.
  • Built for table service. Floor management and order taking on an iPad suit full-service rooms.
  • Modules to add later. Payments, online ordering, reservations, KDS, loyalty, inventory and labor can be switched on when you're ready.

Pricing: The Point of Sale software is $69 a month per location before hardware. TouchBistro's pricing page shows the Essentials bundle, which adds payments and hardware, from $119 monthly, while its homepage has advertised $99. Extras are billed separately and enterprise deals are quoted.
