6. Jolt

Jolt gives restaurants and convenience stores one shift-level app for checklists, food safety logs, prep labels, schedules and staff training. Digi International became the owner in August 2025 and grouped Jolt with its SmartSense business; the Jolt name and website carry on unchanged.

That ownership helps food safety teams, because SmartSense temperature sensors can write into the same logs your crew completes by hand.

Notable customers: A&W Restaurants and White Spot Hospitality.

What's new: Digi paid roughly $145.5 million for Jolt in August 2025. Five months later the combined business unveiled SmartSense ONE on the NRF 2026 show floor, a modular offering where Jolt and SmartSense capabilities are split across seven core modules.

Key features:

  • Proof that tasks got done. A line check or closing list can demand a photo plus manager approval before it's marked complete.
  • Temperature logs. Food safety records are kept in Jolt, and SmartSense sensors can watch walk-ins and coolers without anyone checking by hand.
  • Label printing. Crew members print date codes and prep labels on the spot, which keeps rotation honest.
  • Schedules beside tasks. A drag-and-drop scheduler and a time clock share the app with the checklists.
  • Keeping crews informed. Training content, an information hub and text alerts reach every employee.
  • Store-by-store follow-through. Reports compare task completion across locations, and POS links include PAR Brink.

What makes them stand out: Paper logs become time-stamped records, giving franchise owners evidence that every shift handled its food safety duties.

Pricing: Jolt doesn't list prices; its pricing page routes you to sales for a custom quote.

It's a great fit for: Franchise and quick-service operators who need accountability on every shift and dependable food safety logs, particularly if SmartSense sensors are on the shortlist too.
