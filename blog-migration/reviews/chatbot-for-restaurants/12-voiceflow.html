12. VoiceFlow

Voiceflow, headquartered in Toronto, gives your team a canvas to design AI chatbots and phone agents, try them out and keep them running, combining drawn workflows with LLM playbooks. It ships no restaurant templates or POS connectors, so it suits teams that want to design a custom chatbot themselves without heavy coding.

Notable customers: Trilogy, Turo and Roam.

What's new: In February 2026, Voiceflow unveiled V4, available to all users by March 2026. Agents now mix autonomous playbooks with deterministic visual workflows and tools, route between them, and can assign a different LLM to each part.

What you can do with Voiceflow:

  • Lay out strict steps visually. The canvas handles flows that must go in order, like collecting party size, date and time before a booking request.
  • Write skills in plain language. Playbooks describe a task in natural language, and the agent picks one when a question fits.
  • Run on chat and phone. The same platform builds website and messaging chatbots plus voice agents for your phone line.
  • Ground answers in your content. A knowledge base pulls from your documents and web pages, such as menus and FAQs.
  • Connect your systems. An API, custom functions and integrations link the agent to CRMs, helpdesks and other tools.
  • Test and review as a team. A shared workspace, test runs, transcripts and analytics help several editors improve the agent.

Why Voiceflow stands out: It sits between ready-made chat widgets and developer-first platforms, letting a marketing or CX team build a fairly complex agent visually and choose a fast or reasoning model for each part.

It's best for: Groups where someone on the marketing, digital or guest experience side has time to own the bot and shape how it handles FAQs, bookings and order-status questions.

Pricing: A free plan is available. According to outside reviews, Pro runs near $60 for each editor monthly, Business sits near $150 monthly, and additional seats and usage credits cost more. Enterprise deals are quoted.
