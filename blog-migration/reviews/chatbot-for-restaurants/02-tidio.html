2. Tidio

Tidio puts a chat box on a restaurant's website and staffs it with Lyro, an AI agent that replies to guests using the content you give it. The company sells it as customer service software for smaller businesses, adding Flows for no-code automations and an email help desk, so one small team covers chat and inbox together.

Notable customers: Suitor, Gecko Hospitality and Pastreez.

What's new: In October 2026, Tidio said Lyro was ranked first on G2's AI CX Agent Evaluations page (default view) and scored best for following company policy. Keep in mind that Tidio published this result about itself.

What you can do with Tidio:

  • Cover the repeat questions. Lyro draws on your knowledge base and website to tell guests your hours, whether you take walk-ins or where to park.
  • Hand tricky chats to a person. When a guest has a complaint or a large-party request, Lyro passes the conversation to someone on your team.
  • Set up greetings and lead capture. The visual Flows editor builds welcome messages, FAQ buttons and catering inquiry forms without any code.
  • Reply between tables. Mobile and desktop apps let a manager pick up a live chat from wherever they are in the building.
  • Bring email into the same tool. Guest emails become help desk tickets, which Lyro can also reply to.
  • Check what the AI resolves. Reports show chat volume and how many conversations Lyro closed without help.

Why Tidio stands out: A non-technical owner can point Lyro at the restaurant's website, add a handful of FAQs and try a working website chatbot on the free plan before paying for anything.

It's best for: Owner-run and mid-size restaurants hoping to answer web visitors around the clock, with no coder on the team.

Pricing: You can start free. On a yearly contract, Starter works out to $24.17 a month ($29 if you pay monthly) and Growth begins at $49.17 a month; Plus has custom pricing. Lyro costs extra, based on how many AI conversations you use.
