6. OpenTable

OpenTable, part of Booking Holdings, runs one of the best-known diner reservation networks along with the table management software restaurants use to seat those bookings. Its chatbot is Concierge, a generative AI assistant built into every listed restaurant's page, where diners can ask questions ahead of reserving, so a listed restaurant has one running without extra setup.

Notable customers: Piano Piano (Toronto) and HMSHost.

What's new: August 2026 brought OpenTable's biggest release yet, with 20 or more features added or reworked. Among them: restaurant profiles written by AI, Guest AI suggestions, hooks for partner voice AI and booking from AI assistants, rolled out first to American and Canadian restaurants. In July 2026, Concierge itself expanded so diners can search for restaurants by describing what they want; it had debuted in July 2025.

What you can do with OpenTable:

  • Let Concierge field questions. Diners click to ask about your restaurant on its profile and get an AI-written answer before choosing a time.
  • Generate your profile with AI. OpenTable can draft the restaurant profile from your website and the details you supply.
  • Get booked through AI assistants. A diner asking ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Alexa+ or Google for a table can land on your availability.
  • Automate guest messages. Booking confirmations and pre-visit reminders are sent for you, with email marketing included on higher tiers.
  • Know your guests. Each guest record stores past visits and noted preferences, and reporting covers covers, no-shows and guest feedback across locations.

Why OpenTable stands out: Concierge is attached to a reservation network diners already use, so questions and bookings happen on the same page, and it can link up with AI phone answering partners and with POS software like Square.

It's best for: Full-service and fine-dining restaurants already on OpenTable that want AI answers for diners without adding a separate chatbot.

Pricing: Plans are named Basic, Core and Pro. Each carries a subscription, and guests who book via OpenTable's diner app or site cost an extra fee per head. OpenTable doesn't list the amounts clearly on its site, so get a quote from sales.
