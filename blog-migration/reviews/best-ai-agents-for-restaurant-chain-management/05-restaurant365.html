5. Restaurant365

Restaurant365 runs the back office for multi-unit restaurant management: the general ledger, food cost, labor, payroll and the reports a CFO reads every Monday. Its newest piece, R365 AI, sits on top of the whole P&L, which is why it belongs in a list of AI agents for chains.

Notable customers: Villa Restaurant Group, Wholly Stromboli and Johnson's Landing.

What's new: Restaurant365 used its booth at the NRA Show in May 2026 to launch R365 AI. Operators got AI-driven dashboards, an advisor they can question, new labor management tools and, for early adopters only, AI-built schedules. A month later, Kate Hastings joined as Chief Customer Officer.

Key features:

  • Accounting built for restaurants. Invoice processing and bank reconciliation run automatically, and P&Ls can be split by entity.
  • Food cost control. Counts and recipe costs show the gap between what each store should have used and what it actually used.
  • Labor matched to demand. Schedules are built against sales forecasts, so staffing follows the traffic you expect.
  • Payroll for hourly teams. Payroll and HR are set up for hourly restaurant staff, tips included.
  • Budgets across units. Area and brand budgets build up from each store, and franchisors can track royalties in the same reports.
  • An AI advisor on the P&L. R365 AI dashboards and the advisor point to cost, labor and margin trends worth a closer look.

What makes them stand out: Accounting and operations share one system, and POS integrations post each day's sales into the books automatically. A multi-unit group can compare location P&Ls without exporting data between tools.

Pricing: Quotes come per location, as bundles you pay for each quarter or each year, and bigger groups negotiate their own terms. Restaurant365 lists more than one set of figures across its site, so ask for a current quote.

It's a great fit for: Operators running several units, corporate or franchised, who want the books, food cost and labor feeding a single P&L.

Worth noting: Reviewers mention a steep learning curve and a setup that takes time, and some find the interface dated. Budget for training when you roll it out across locations.
