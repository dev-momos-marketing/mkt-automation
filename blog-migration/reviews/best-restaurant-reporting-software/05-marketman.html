5. MarketMan

Category: Food cost & inventory reporting

MarketMan handles the back-of-house cost side of a restaurant in the cloud: stock counts, recipe costs, supplier orders and invoices that get read automatically. Its reports answer one question operators ask every week: where did the food money go?

Because ingredient prices update from scanned invoices, recipe costs and margins in your reports reflect what you paid last week, which makes item-level margin reporting far more reliable than a spreadsheet updated once a quarter.

Notable customers: Westville Restaurant Group, ATFC, Ltd. and Taco del Mar (multi-unit franchisee).

What's new: Square sellers gained a built-in option in April 2026, when Square Restaurant Inventory by MarketMan launched globally. It brings waste logs, invoice scanning, vendor records, purchase orders and ingredient and recipe management into Square itself.

Key features:

  • Counts by location. Real-time inventory tracking shows stock on hand at each site, which is the base for any variance or usage report.
  • Live recipe costs. Each dish is priced out using what its ingredients cost today, so a supplier increase shows up in your margins straight away.
  • Price-change alerts. Invoice scanning updates prices automatically and warns you when a product costs more than it did on the last delivery.
  • Waste and loss trends. Logged waste builds into trend reports, helping you separate spoilage and over-portioning from theft or counting errors.
  • COGS across the group. Multi-location reports compare food cost and cost of goods sold by site, with POS and accounting integrations, including Square, feeding the data.

What makes them stand out: MarketMan concentrates on food cost control, so its reports point to the specific items and sites leaking margin well before the month closes.

Pricing: MarketMan doesn't publish current prices; request a quote for your number of locations.

It's a great fit for: Restaurant groups that want tighter food cost margins and regular reporting on waste.
