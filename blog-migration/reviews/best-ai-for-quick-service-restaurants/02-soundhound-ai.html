2. Soundhound AI

Category: Voice AI for phone, drive-thru and kiosk ordering

For a QSR, SoundHound's AI acts as an extra order taker who never misses a call or a car. The publicly traded company (Nasdaq: SOUN) moved its restaurant agents onto OASYS in May 2026, so one agent can be set up and then reused on phones, speakers and kiosks. Buying SYNQ3 in 2024 and Interactions in 2025 expanded its restaurant and customer-service reach.

Notable customers: Five Guys, Chipotle, Red Lobster and Applebee's / IHOP, and Church's Texas Chicken.

What's new: In January 2026, Five Guys extended its partnership after the AI had handled more than one million phone ordering interactions, and individual franchisees may now add AI phone ordering to their restaurants. In August 2025, SoundHound announced deals with Red Lobster, Peter Piper Pizza and Applebee's / IHOP plus a wider Chipotle rollout.

Key features:

  • Phone orders without a cashier. The agent picks up calls, takes the full order and answers questions on the menu, opening hours and allergens.
  • Drive-thru voice ordering. AI takes orders at the speaker post, and it also works with Acrelec drive-thru hardware.
  • Orders land in the POS. Tickets go straight to the kitchen, so crew don't re-enter what the AI heard.
  • Prompts for add-ons. The agent suggests upsells and current promotions while the guest is still ordering.
  • Voice Insights. Drive-thru conversations become operational data you can review across stores.

What makes them stand out: One vendor covers phone, drive-thru, kiosk and in-car ordering, so a QSR brand can run the same AI agent across every ordering channel it has.

Pricing: SoundHound doesn't publish prices; quotes are custom.

It's a great fit for: Large QSR and casual-dining chains with heavy call and drive-thru volume that want one AI ordering system rolled out across hundreds of stores.
