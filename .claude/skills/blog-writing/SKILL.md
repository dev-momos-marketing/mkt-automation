# Writing a Momos blog post

A Momos blog post is the most useful article on its topic for the people who run restaurants: owners, operators, marketers and guest experience teams at multi-location brands. It should leave them with numbers they can trust, steps they can act on this week, and a clear idea of where Momos fits.

Every post has three jobs:

1. **Deliver on the title quickly.** The introduction says what the reader will get, and the first sections answer the question in the title.
2. **Be more practical than any other page.** Real data with named sources, restaurant examples, worked numbers, and at least one interactive calculator where something can be calculated.
3. **Help the reader act.** Each section ends with what to do; the post ends with where to start, software picks and an FAQ.

## Voice

### Clear, practical and conversational

Write like an experienced operator advising a capable peer: confident, specific and warm.

- **Explain why before what.** Say why a tactic works, then how to do it.
- **Be specific.** "A card in the delivery bag with a first-order offer" beats "encourage direct orders".
- **Speak to the reader as "you".** Use "we" for Momos, sparingly. The author may use "I" occasionally where the post already does.
- **Vary sentence length**, 12–22 words on average, and split anything over 30.
- **Contractions are welcome** (you'll, it's, don't).
- **American English**: behavior, optimize, favorite, color.

Imperatives are fine in step lists ("Fill in every field.") because the reader is doing something. In running text, prefer explaining ("Profiles with complete hours and photos get more visits, because…").

### Never use "X, not Y" constructions

Banned everywhere, including headings and FAQs: "X, not Y", "X — not Y", "not X, but Y", "not just X, but Y", "isn't X; it's Y". State what something is and why it matters.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| The issue is not headcount, but menu complexity. | The issue is menu complexity slowing down execution. |
| It's not just a score, it's a process. | The score comes with a routine for following up with guests. |

### No hype, no filler

Avoid "in today's fast-paced world", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "supercharge", "seamless", "robust", "delight", "And guess what?". Promotional language belongs only in the software section and the embeds that already carry a call to action.

### Be honest about uncertainty

Say when a figure is a rule of thumb, comes from one small study, or depends on the market. "The study was small, run in one restaurant, but it is easy and free to test" is better than overstating it.

## Facts and sourcing

This is the rule that matters most. Older posts are full of numbers nobody can trace ("up to 90%", "93% of consumers", "109 seconds", "boost sales by 27%"); remove those or replace them with sourced ones.

- **Never invent a number.** Every statistic, benchmark, date and study must be real and current. If you can't verify it, describe it qualitatively or leave it out.
- **Name the source in the sentence**, with the year: "In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 97% of consumers said…".
- **Prefer primary sources**: the publisher's own report or page over a blog that quotes it. Check at least two independent sources when you can't open the primary one, and tell the person which numbers you couldn't open yourself.
- **Say what a figure measures**: a median pre-tax margin, an average rating and a share of respondents are different things.
- **Check every calculation**, including worked examples and calculator defaults.
- **Company examples** (Chipotle, Starbucks, Chick-fil-A) stay qualitative unless you have a source for the number.

## Structure

Keep the number in the title. The title lives in its own CMS field and isn't changed by the body: a post titled "17 Practical Strategies" must have exactly 17 numbered strategies.

### Every post

| Part | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Key takeaways box | 3–5 one-sentence takeaways at the very top (see "Key takeaways box"). |
| Introduction (no heading) | Two or three short paragraphs on the problem and why it matters, then "In this guide, you'll find:" with 4–5 bullets. |
| Data section | "X in 2026: What the Data Says": 3–4 sourced figures and what they mean for the reader. |
| Body | Numbered H2s for list posts, or "What is / How it works / How to" H2s for guides. Each section explains why, then gives steps as a list with **bold lead-ins**. |
| Calculator | At least one wherever there is something to calculate (revenue, cost, savings, ratings). Place it right after the paragraph that sets it up. |
| How to measure X | The 4–6 numbers that show whether it's working, with what each one means. |
| Where to start | A short ordered list: this week, this month, this quarter, ongoing. |
| Best software for X | Three picks (see below). |
| FAQ | "X: FAQ" as an H2, 4–6 questions as H3s, answered in 2–4 sentences each. Use questions people actually search. |

### Headings

- Keep the keyword in most H2s ("How To Measure Restaurant Sales Growth"). Blog H2s use Title Case like the rest of the blog; numbered strategy H2s start with the number ("1. Optimize your Google Business Profile").
- H3s for steps, software picks and FAQ questions.
- Never an H1: the title is the post's H1.

### Readability

- Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences, at most about 50 words.
- No more than four paragraphs in a row without a heading, list, image or embed.
- Lists: bold lead-in, then one or two sentences. Keep bullets parallel.
- No emoji, decorative dividers or blockquote tips.

## The software section

Three picks, each as an H3 with one intro sentence and three bullets: **Best for** (naming the strategies it serves), **Key features**, **Why it helps**.

- **Momos first when it genuinely fits the topic.** Describe only what Momos does (below).
- **The other two are well-known, relevant products**, described neutrally from their own product pages. No invented features, no prices unless you've verified them, and no links to competitors' websites.
- Mention material facts a reader would want, such as ownership (SevenRooms is owned by DoorDash since 2025).
- A post that compares many tools (a "Best X software" post) can list more; link it from other posts instead of repeating it.

## Calculators and other embeds

A post can't hold a component, so an interactive module is an embed: its code lives in the post's Embeds field, and a line `<p>⟦embed:ID⟧</p>` in the text marks where BlogEmbeds puts it.

- **Write it as** `blog-migration/posts/<slug>.embeds/<ID>.html`. The ID is letters, digits, `_` or `-`, unique in the post (for example `revLevers`). Blog write adds it to the Embeds field, or replaces the embed with that ID, and keeps the others.
- **Wrap everything in one element with an id** and scope all CSS to it (`#rev-levers .v {…}`). Start the `<style>` with `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Poppins:wght@400;500;600&display=swap');` and `#id,#id *{box-sizing:border-box;margin:0}`.
- **Brand colors** (the same as the existing embeds): red `#D73E33`, ink `#15181E`, body gray `#353D4D`, tint background `#FDF7F6`, input border `#E3DCDA`, white cards. Labels 13px, inputs 16px, results 22px semibold, the key result in red.
- **Layout**: a small red uppercase "Calculator" label, a one-line question as the title, inputs in a grid (`repeat(auto-fit,minmax(120px,1fr))`, labels short enough for one line), results in a white card, then one or two lines explaining the formula and naming the source.
- **Put the initial results in the HTML** and make the script compute exactly the same text for the default inputs. Handle empty and impossible inputs with a friendly message.
- **Scripts**: an embed with a `<script>` runs in a sandboxed frame that grows to fit; it can't read the page. Use plain ES5-style JavaScript that only touches its own element ids. An embed without a script is placed inline in a shadow root, so page styles don't reach it.
- **Check it** in a browser at 1280 and 390 px wide (Playwright is installed), including typing into an input.
- Keep a post's existing embeds (their IDs are in the live page's `data-embed-source` attributes and in the post's current Content) unless the person asks to remove one.

## HTML a post may contain

The body is HTML from the first H2-level content on: `<h2>`, `<h3>`, `<p>`, `<ul>`, `<ol>`, `<li>`, `<strong>`, `<em>`, `<a>`, `<blockquote>`, `<br>`, `<img>`, and `<p>⟦embed:ID⟧</p>` lines. No `<div>`, classes or inline styles: anything visual goes in an embed.

- **Lists are always native** `<ol>`/`<ul>`: BlogEmbeds styles their numbers to match the blog. Never draw a numbered list inside an embed; an embed is for what text can't do (calculators, tables, visuals). Older posts' list embeds are converted with **Blog replace embeds** (`blog-migration/replacements/`).
- **Images**: keep the post's existing images that still fit (their framerusercontent.com URLs are in the live page). Write them as `<img src="https://framerusercontent.com/images/….png" alt="What it shows, in words" width="…" height="…">`, placed after the paragraph they illustrate.
- **Links**: internal ones as paths (`/blog/slug`, `/glossary/slug`, `/product/…`); external ones as full URLs with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`.

## Links

- **8–15 links per post**, of which **5 or fewer** go to other websites.
- **At most one per paragraph or list item, each URL once**, at its most useful mention, with descriptive anchor text from the sentence.
- **Internal**: glossary terms at first mention, related blog posts, and the Momos product page or case study for what's being described. Only pages that exist: Blog write checks every internal link against the sitemap and refuses to save a broken one.
- **External**: only the source behind a number you quote, or a policy the reader must follow. Never competitors' websites.
- The **Blog links** workflow adds links to posts in Framer only. A post written from `posts/` is overwritten from its file, so add links in the file.

## Workflow

1. **Read the live post first**: fetch `https://www.momos.com/blog/<slug>` and pull out its text, images, embed IDs (`data-embed-source`) and links. The embed code is in the page's hidden `data-embed-source` blocks; scripts' embeds are in the Embeds field.
2. **Research** current figures (WebSearch), and record what you verified and how.
3. **Write** `blog-migration/posts/<slug>.html` (and any `posts/<slug>.embeds/<ID>.html`), one block per line.
4. **Check**:

```bash
cd blog-migration && npm test
```

   The tests check that every file in `posts/` is well-formed HTML. Render a preview with the embeds and look at it at 1280 and 390 px.
5. **Commit and open a PR** on the session's branch. After it's merged, the person runs **Actions → Blog write** with the slug, first as a dry run (it lists words before and after, embeds, links, and any problem), then with **apply**. It saves the English Content (and new embeds), reads it back, and marks existing translations "needs review" in Framer's localization.
6. **A person publishes in Framer.** Scripts never publish.

## FAQs for existing posts

A post that isn't written from `posts/` gets its FAQ from `blog-migration/faqs/<slug>.json` (`{"heading": "Topic: FAQ", "questions": [{"q", "a"}]}`), which the **Blog FAQs** workflow appends to its Content. Read the live post first, ground every answer in it, and use no number the post doesn't contain (the workflow refuses one). Plain text only, 4–6 questions, 2–4 sentences each.

## Key takeaways box

Every post opens with a "Key takeaways" box: 3–5 one-sentence takeaways (at most 35 words each) that answer the title and give the key steps. It's the `keyTakeaways` embed built by `scripts/takeaways.mjs`, so the look stays the same on every post. For a post in Framer, write `blog-migration/takeaways/<slug>.json` and run **Blog takeaways**. For a post written from `posts/`, put `<p>⟦embed:keyTakeaways⟧</p>` first in the file and generate `posts/<slug>.embeds/keyTakeaways.html` with `takeawaysEmbed()`.

## Checklist before you commit

1. The title's promise is met, and any number in the title matches the numbered sections.
2. The introduction says what the reader gets; there's a sourced data section.
3. Every number has a named source and year, or was computed; the arithmetic checks out.
4. No "X, not Y" constructions, no hype words, American spelling.
5. Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences; lists use bold lead-ins.
6. At least one calculator where something can be calculated, with default results that match the script, checked at 1280 and 390 px.
7. Three software picks, Momos only where it fits and described within the list above, no competitor links.
8. 8–15 links, at most 5 external, one per paragraph, each URL once.
9. Existing images and embeds kept unless they no longer fit.
10. How to measure, where to start and an FAQ are there.
11. `npm test` passes.
