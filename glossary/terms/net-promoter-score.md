Net Promoter Score asks guests one question: *"How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?"* on a scale from 0 to 10.

## How NPS is calculated

Guests are grouped by their answer:

- **Promoters (9–10):** loyal fans who are likely to come back and recommend you.
- **Passives (7–8):** satisfied, but open to trying a competitor.
- **Detractors (0–6):** unhappy guests who may warn others away.

**NPS = % promoters − % detractors.** Passives count toward the total but not either group, so the score ranges from -100 (all detractors) to 100 (all promoters).

For example, out of 200 responses with 120 promoters, 50 passives and 30 detractors: 60% − 15% = an NPS of **45**.

## Why NPS matters for restaurants

Word of mouth and online reviews drive where people eat. NPS gives a single number to track loyalty across locations and over time, and the follow-up question ("What's the main reason for your score?") tells you what to fix.

## Getting more out of NPS

- Ask soon after the visit, while the experience is fresh.
- Always add an open-text follow-up question; the comments are where the actionable detail is.
- Compare locations against each other, not just against the brand average.
- Close the loop: reply to detractors quickly and fix what they raise.
