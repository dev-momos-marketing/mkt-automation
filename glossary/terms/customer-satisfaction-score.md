Customer Satisfaction Score measures how happy guests are with a specific experience: a meal, a delivery, a call to the restaurant. It's usually a single question such as *"How satisfied were you with your visit today?"* answered on a 1–5 scale.

## How CSAT is calculated

**CSAT = (satisfied responses ÷ total responses) × 100**, where "satisfied" usually means a 4 or 5.

If 340 of 400 guests answer 4 or 5, your CSAT is **85%**.

## CSAT vs. NPS

CSAT is about one moment; [NPS](/glossary/net-promoter-score) is about the relationship with the brand. A guest can be satisfied with tonight's dinner (high CSAT) but still not recommend you over a favourite spot (passive on NPS). Tracking both shows the difference between a good visit and a loyal guest.

## Tips for useful CSAT data

- Keep the survey short: one rating plus one optional comment.
- Ask about one touchpoint at a time (food, service, wait time, delivery).
- Break results down by location, daypart and channel to see where problems start.
- Read low scores alongside the comments and public reviews to find the cause.
